Privacy Policy

Application: GetInvoice (Invoice → FedEx)
Effective date: October 5, 2026
Maintainer: Shawn Zhai
Contact: pxiangZhai@gmail.com

1. Scope

This policy explains how GetInvoice, an independently maintained desktop utility for designated family members and authorized internal users, handles information. The maintainer is the individual identified above. This policy covers the utility and identifies third-party services involved in its operation.

2. Information processed

  • QuickBooks information: the utility requests invoice records for the date range you select and related customer records. API responses may include invoice identifiers, dates, line items, amounts, customer names, company names, addresses, telephone numbers, email addresses, and other fields returned by QuickBooks, even when those fields are not included in the exported CSV.
  • Shipping information: recipient and package information entered or edited by you, such as addresses, weight, dimensions, service type, contents, and declared customs value.
  • Connection information: Intuit application credentials, access and refresh tokens, and the connected company identifier. Your QuickBooks password and verification codes are entered on Intuit's official pages, not collected through the utility's forms.

3. Purposes

Information is used to authorize and maintain the company connection, retrieve selected invoice and customer records, prepare shipping information for your review, and generate a CSV file at your request. The utility includes no advertising or analytics integration. The maintainer does not sell information obtained through the utility.

4. Local processing and storage

The current desktop implementation processes invoice, customer, mapping, and shipping information in memory on the user's computer. It does not intentionally save raw API responses to a local database. Working records and mappings are cleared when the process exits or the corresponding records are replaced. Application credentials and authorization tokens are stored in the operating system credential store and remain until removed. Credentials and a bootstrap refresh token may initially be entered into the local config.json file. The utility clears the client secret from that file after saving it to the credential store, and clears the bootstrap company identifier and refresh token after a successful authorization import. Until those steps succeed, the file may contain sensitive information; keep it private and delete any copies or backups separately. Exported CSV files and any copies remain wherever you save them; the utility does not delete them. Browser caches, operating system swap, backups, and other system features may retain copies outside the utility's control.

5. Transfers and third-party services

The utility communicates with Intuit over HTTPS for authorization, token refresh, API requests, and revocation. The production authorization workflow is described below. You choose whether to upload an exported CSV to FedEx or another service; the current utility does not upload CSV files to a carrier automatically. No feature automatically sends invoice records or CSV exports to the maintainer.

Production authorization: For the production workflow described by this policy, the company administrator completes authorization in Intuit’s official OAuth 2.0 Playground, using Intuit’s hosted callback at https://developer.intuit.com/v2/OAuth2Playground/RedirectUrl. Intuit handles the authorization callback and token exchange. The authorized user then provides the company identifier and refresh token through the desktop utility’s local configuration file. This workflow uses no maintainer-operated public callback server or third-party tunnel. Intuit’s handling of information is governed by its Privacy Statement.

Policy-page hosting: GitHub Pages (GitHub), governed by the GitHub General Privacy Statement. The policy pages contain no application analytics or customer records. The hosting provider may process visitor IP addresses and access logs under its own policy. Third-party privacy practices are governed by the relevant providers' policies.

6. Security

The local interface binds to a loopback address and uses a launch token, session checks, and protection for form submissions. The built-in local authorization callback used for sandbox connections checks a short-lived, single-use authorization state; production authorization through the Playground is handled by Intuit. These measures reduce risk but cannot guarantee absolute security. Use a trusted computer and user account, keep software updated, and restrict access to credentials and exported files.

7. Revocation and deletion

Use the utility's disconnect action to request revocation from Intuit. On success, the utility clears its stored authorization tokens and current working records; application configuration remains. If revocation fails, retry or remove the connection through QuickBooks. Exiting the utility alone does not revoke authorization.

To remove all local application configuration, delete the entries for the credential-store service InvoiceToFedEx-v1 (configuration and tokens) using your operating system's credential-management tools. Revoke the connection before deleting tokens if possible. Separately delete exported CSV files, copies, and backups according to your business requirements. Deleting local information does not delete invoices or customer records in QuickBooks or shipments already submitted to a carrier.

8. Requests and contact

Contact the maintainer at the email above with questions or requests relating to the utility. The maintainer does not centrally hold your locally processed invoice records or exports and may need you to carry out local deletion. Requests concerning the company's source records should also be directed to the company or the relevant service provider.

9. Changes

This policy will be updated when the utility's data handling changes, with a revised effective date. Review it before using a materially changed version, particularly if a hosted authorization service or automated carrier upload is added.

10. Local diagnostic logs

The utility keeps bounded diagnostic logs on the user’s computer to help troubleshoot problems. These contain UTC timestamps, operation categories, HTTP status codes, Intuit response tracking identifiers (intuit_tid), numeric API error codes, standardized fault or OAuth error categories, and general local or network error categories. They do not intentionally contain customer records, company or invoice identifiers, raw API responses, request URLs or parameters, application secrets, authorization codes, or authorization tokens. The utility retains up to three files of approximately 1 MiB each, removing the oldest during rotation. If disk logging is unavailable, up to 1,000 recent entries are retained in memory for the current session. Users may export logs and choose to share them for troubleshooting; the utility does not upload them automatically. To delete stored logs, remove the local logs folder and separately delete exported copies and backups.
